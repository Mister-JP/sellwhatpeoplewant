# Home and About content inventory

## Governing promise

- SellWhatPeopleWant publishes business research that an ordinary person can
  understand, question, and verify without prior training in economics or business.
- The primary product is the Opportunity Case: a clear, evidence-backed,
  continuously correctable account of whether a business may work for particular
  people, in a particular place, under particular conditions.
- Every case must do two jobs. It must help with the decision in front of the reader,
  and it must help that reader understand the sources, concepts, calculations, and
  questions well enough to think more capably elsewhere.
- The clear account comes first. Exact sources, deeper concepts, historical
  development, formulas, alternatives, and disagreements remain one deliberate step
  away instead of becoming an entrance exam.
- Familiar human experience is the starting language: what people need, choose,
  avoid, pay, wait for, repair, give up, risk, and do together.
- A necessary technical term is explained in place and connected to a deeper path.
  The platform does not invent a named method vocabulary that readers must learn.
- A calculation begins with the real relationship it is trying to represent. The
  exact formula, inputs, assumptions, sensitivity, and limits stay inspectable.
- A source is not made trustworthy by a badge or unexplained score. The case says who
  produced it, what they could know, why it helps here, what shaped it, and what it
  cannot establish.
- Widely accepted knowledge is useful shared ground, not permanent truth. Where it
  matters, a reader can see how it became accepted, how it changed, where it travels,
  and what remains disputed.
- The conclusion remains conditional. A polished publication must still show human
  judgment, competing explanations, important unknowns, and what would change the
  answer.
- The reader should leave able to explain the central case in their own words, check
  an important claim, understand what a key number means, see a serious uncertainty,
  and ask a better next question.

## Important distinctions

- Behaviour often reveals constraints and trade-offs that statements alone miss.
  Spending is evidence, not a magical window into one hidden true preference.
- Accessibility does not mean removing rigor. The site layers it: ordinary meaning
  first, exact detail underneath.
- Expertise is real. The platform makes expert reasoning traversable by exposing the
  fundamentals, observations, practice, assumptions, and disagreements that support
  it; it does not pretend experience contributes nothing.
- Mathematics is not the enemy. Unexplained mathematical authority is. A reader may
  rely on an exact model without being required to derive it, but must be able to see
  what it represents and which assumptions control the result.
- Public internet sources are not the whole world. Field observations, practitioner
  knowledge, paid or restricted material, and local records may matter; their access
  and limits must be stated honestly.
- A useful case can end with no, not yet, only under these conditions, or insufficient
  evidence. Teaching the reader must not turn a weak opportunity into a positive one.

## Present-stage facts

- No real Opportunity Case has been published in the public library yet.
- The first stage is one geography, one bounded opportunity family, and a small
  portfolio of deeply researched, visually excellent cases.
- The current pages communicate and exercise the reader promise; they do not claim
  that the research practice has been externally validated.
- Open collaboration, implementation services, financing information, and a
  real-world business simulator are later stages rather than current features.
- The homepage does not claim customers, adoption, validated opportunities, or
  realized business outcomes.

## Page outline

### Chapter one — what this is for

1. Begin with a recognizable human situation: people routinely make choices through
   work, purchases, waiting, substitution, repair, and compromise.
2. State the promise without category language: we investigate possible businesses,
   explain what we understand, show how we reached it, and keep the unfinished parts
   visible.
3. Introduce the Opportunity Case as a decision aid and public learning path.
4. Use one clearly hypothetical business question to show that a case follows people,
   alternatives, money, practical work, rules, downside, and the next test.
5. Make clear that words and behaviour are both evidence shaped by circumstances.

A scroll-driven Three.js research world may carry this chapter. Shape and color can
distinguish sources, interpretations, constraints, and unknowns, but the visual remains
illustrative and makes no market or confidence claim.

### Editorial break

The break should express the philosophy in ordinary words: a conclusion becomes
useful when a reader can see how people reached it and where it could still be wrong.

### Chapter two — how understanding opens

6. Demonstrate the reading layers: clear explanation, linked source, concept path,
   calculation meaning, disagreement, open question, and change history.
7. Explain why a source helps and what it cannot show, rather than displaying an
   unexplained evidence grade.
8. Explain an important business concept through a familiar situation, then make its
   name, history, use, limits, and continuing discussion available for deeper study.
9. Explain the purpose of a calculation before its formula and let the reader see how
   changed assumptions create changed outcomes.
10. Show why the research question itself is a choice and name plausible questions
    that would have produced a different investigation.
11. Close with disagreements, unknowns, and the still-in-research case-library
    boundary. The destination is independent judgment, not borrowed certainty.

The GPU-IO fluid simulation may carry this chapter as an isolated decorative system
with a static fallback. It does not encode evidence, confidence, or product state.

## Language test

Before publishing a section, ask:

- Can a person unfamiliar with the field say what this means after one reading?
- Did we explain why the idea or number matters before naming or formalizing it?
- Can the reader reach the original support and understand why we used it?
- Did we expose the main assumption, disagreement, and unknown without hiding the
  conclusion?
- Does this give the reader a useful next question rather than merely asking for
  trust?
